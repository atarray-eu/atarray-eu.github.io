Regional experiments

Over the past decades many regional ocean-bottom seismomter (OBS) experiments were carried out in the Atlantic Ocean that had regional or even local scientific foci. The design of these OBS networks were adjusted to very specific scientific objectives, resulting in either more linear configurations or limited aperture. Below, you find information about a past and ongoing regional deployments.

UPFLOW 2021-2022 - Upward mantle flow from novel seismic observations

The UPFLOW project aims to constrain plume-like mantle upwellings at unprecedented resolution using novel seismic imaging approaches. Upward mantle flow is critical for continental growth, for returning volatiles to the atmosphere, and for producing Earth’s largest melting events forming large igneous provinces. These melting events often coincide with major extinctions, supercontinent breakups and with changes in geodynamo behaviour and therefore magnetic field of the Earth. During the period from July/August 20221 till August/September 2022, 50 broadband OBS were deployed across the Azores-Madeira-Canary islands region.

PIs: Ana Ferreira, UCL London, United Kingdom; Miguel Miranda, IPMA Lissabon, Portugal

Funder: European Research Council, ERC.

Project website: https://upflow-eu.github.io/

VoiLA 2016-2017 - Volatile Recycling in the Lesser Antilles

The VoiLA project focused on volatile cycling in the Lesser Antilles subduction zone. Between March 2016 and May 2017, 34 broadband OBS were deployed around the Lesser Antilles arc, forearc, and backarc. The experiment investigated how water and other volatiles stored in the incoming Atlantic plate get released during subduction and transported through the mantle wedge toward the volcanic arc.

PIs: Saskia Goes, ImperialCollege London, United Kingdom.

Funder: Natural Environment Research Council, grant NE/KO10743/1

Project information: Project VoiLA: The Volatile Recycling in the Lesser Antilles

PI-LAB 2016-2017 - Passive Imaging of the Lithosphere-Asthenosphere Boundary

The PI-LAB project focused on imaging the oceanic lithosphere and asthenosphere near the equatorial Mid-Atlantic Ridge. The experiment combined passive seismic observations from 30 broadband OBS, seismic reflection and refraction profiles (AS), and 20 ocean-bottom magnetotelluric (MT) stations to investigate what defines the base of a tectonic plate and how the lithosphere-asthenosphere boundary varies beneath young Atlantic seafloor.

PIs: Nicholas Harmon, NOC Southampton, United Kingdom; Kate Rychert, NOC Southampton, United Kingdom.

Funder: Natural Environment Research Council, NERC.

Project website: Passive Imaging of the Lithosphere-Asthenosphere Boundary, PI-LAB

NEAREST 2007-2008 - Integrated observations from NEAR shore sourcES of Tsunamis: towards an early warning system

The NEAREST project addressed the identification and characterisation of large potential tsunami sources located near shore in the Gulf of Cadiz. Major objectives were the improvement of near real-time detection of signals by a multi parameter seafloor observatory for the characterisation of potential tsunamigenic sources to be used in the development of an Early Warning System (EWS) Prototype, the improvement of integrated numerical models enabling more accurate scenarios of tsunami impact and the production of accurate inundation maps in selected areas of the Algarve (SW Portugal), highly hit by the 1755 tsunamis. In addition to the multi parameter seafloor observatory, 24 OBS were deployed for about 12 months from 2007 till 2008.

PIs: Nevio Zitellini, Istituto di Scienze Marine (ISMAR), Bologna, Italy.

Funder: European Union, grant ID 37110.

Project website: NEAREST 2007-2008 - Integrated observations from NEAR shore sourcES of Tsunamis: towards an early warning system
