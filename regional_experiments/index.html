Regional experiments

UPFLOW, 2021-2022

While downward mantle flow, subduction, is well constrained, a grand challenge in Earth sciences is to understand mid-plate, deep upward flow that cannot be explained by plate tectonics. The aim of the UPFLOW project, funded by the European Research Council, 2021-2026, is to develop an entirely new seismic imaging approach and to use it to constrain plume-like mantle upwellings at unprecedented resolution. Upward flow is critical for continental growth, for returning volatiles to the atmosphere and for producing Earth’s largest melting events. These events coincide with major extinctions, supercontinent breakups and with changes in geodynamo behaviour.

The project included two very successful ocean-bottom seismometer campaigns in July-August 2021, during which 50 OBSs were deployed across the Azores-Madeira-Canary region.

Funder: European Research Council, ERC.

Project website: https://upflow-eu.github.io/

VoiLA, 2016-2017

VoiLA, Volatile Recycling in the Lesser Antilles, was a NERC-funded project focused on volatile cycling in the Lesser Antilles subduction zone. Between March 2016 and May 2017, the project deployed 34 broadband ocean-bottom seismometers, BBOBS, on the Lesser Antilles arc, forearc, and backarc. The experiment investigated how water and other volatiles are stored in the incoming Atlantic plate, released during subduction, and transported through the mantle wedge toward the volcanic arc.

Funder: Natural Environment Research Council, NERC.

Project information: Project VoiLA: The Volatile Recycling in the Lesser Antilles

PI-LAB, 2016-2017

PI-LAB, Passive Imaging of the Lithosphere-Asthenosphere Boundary, was a NERC-funded project focused on imaging the oceanic lithosphere and asthenosphere near the equatorial Mid-Atlantic Ridge. The experiment combined passive seismic observations from 30 broadband ocean-bottom seismometers, OBS, shown by red triangles, active seismic, AS, reflection and refraction profiles, shown by dashed lines, and 20 magnetotelluric, MT, stations, shown by X symbols and dashed lines. These observations were used to investigate what defines the base of a tectonic plate and how the lithosphere-asthenosphere boundary varies beneath young Atlantic seafloor.

Funder: Natural Environment Research Council, NERC.

Project website: Passive Imaging of the Lithosphere-Asthenosphere Boundary, PI-LAB
